# Compliance Templates

**Doc Manager → Compliance** holds pre-configured **retention schedules** for different
document types and industries — so "how long do we keep this?" is answered once and applied
consistently.

## Why templates rather than per-document

Retention set per document drifts. Two people file the same kind of contract a month apart
and pick different periods; a year later nobody can say which is right. A template attaches
the period to the **kind** of record, so every document of that type inherits it.

It also means a policy change is made in one place.

## Creating one

**Create Template**, then define the document type it applies to and the retention period.

Templates are the input to [Retention & Disposal](/dms/retention): when a document's
retention period elapses, it appears there as due for review.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Retention periods are a legal and policy question, not a technical one.** HubSign will
  apply whatever schedule you give it. The periods appropriate to your records depend on
  your jurisdiction, industry and any live litigation or audit. Have them confirmed by
  whoever owns records policy before relying on them.
</Callout>

## What a schedule needs to say

At minimum, for each document type:

- **How long** it must be kept
- **From when** — creation, or a business event such as contract end or final payment
- **What happens next** — destroy, or archive indefinitely

The "from when" is the part most often left vague and the part that causes the most
trouble later. *Seven years from the end of the contract* and *seven years from filing*
are different dates, and only one of them is what a regulator asked for.

## A practical starting point

Rather than templating everything at once, template the categories where you have the most
volume and the clearest obligation — typically finance records. Leave low-volume or
uncertain categories out until their policy is settled; an unclear schedule applied to
thousands of records is worse than no schedule.
